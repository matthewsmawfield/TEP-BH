4. Existence Demonstrations

The Schwarzschild incompatibility result determines the admissible class of strong-field solutions: the temporal field must backreact on the geometric metric, and the resulting geometry must simultaneously achieve finite matter-frame curvature, continuous temporal transport, real exterior hair, and dynamical backreaction. The Schwarzschild obstruction is not a general geometric impossibility. Regular geometry, bounded matter-frame curvature, and dynamical scalar backreaction are each explicitly attainable — established separately by the two demonstrations below. Their joint realisation in one dynamically generated solution is the nonlinear closure selected by the TEP action.

4.1 Regular Geometry Exists

The Hayward metric $F(r) = 1 - 2Mr^2/(r^3 + 2M\ell^2)$ is a regular geometric background: the Kretschmann scalar is finite everywhere, $K_{\rm Hayward}(0) = 24/\ell^4$, and the effective energy density is finite, $\rho_{\rm eff} \to 3/\ell^2$ at the centre. The radial equation of state is $w_r = -1$ — cosmological-constant-like — with anisotropic tangential pressure. This profile is the signature of a higher-curvature coupling: a scalar field's stress-energy, modified by the Gauss-Bonnet invariant, can in principle produce anisotropic effective pressure with a de Sitter-like radial component.

The demonstration shows that singularity is optional, not mandatory. Once the geometric singularity is absent, the TEP matter metric simultaneously achieves bounded areal radius and finite Kretschmann (Section 5, Appendix K). The burden shifts: GR must justify why the singular reconstruction is mandatory, not why TEP must reproduce every deep-region coordinate chart. The Hayward metric is one example of a regular geometry; it is not the TEP-selected solution.

4.2 Dynamical Coupling Exists

The scalar-Gauss-Bonnet (sGB) coupling $\alpha_{\rm GB}\,\phi\,\mathcal{G}$ is the leading curvature operator of the strong-field EFT (Section 2.1). It is the lowest-dimension higher-curvature term that couples the temporal field to the gravitational geometry and supplies real backreaction. The published perturbative solution (Sotiriou \& Zhou 2014, exact $\mathcal{O}(\alpha^2)$) establishes:

  • Scalar hair around strong-field sources — the no-hair theorem is evaded because the Gauss-Bonnet coupling provides a geometric source term.
  • Real backreaction on the gravitational metric — the temporal field is not epiphenomenal.
  • Sector-dependent observables — the shadow is sensitive to the geometric metric at $\mathcal{O}(\eta^2)$ while the ISCO feels the conformal factor at $\mathcal{O}(\eta)$, because null geodesics are conformally invariant while timelike geodesics are not (Appendix L).

What sGB does not establish: a regular deep region. The nonlinear solutions of Sotiriou \& Zhou (2014) for the same linear sGB coupling develop a finite-area singularity rather than a regular temporal minimum, and recent simulations (Thaalba et al. 2024) confirm this and explore a possible connection to loss of hyperbolicity. The TEP-selected solution must satisfy the minimal temporal-well criteria: $0 < N(r)$ and $N_{\min} \ll N_o$ everywhere, with no observer-independent one-way boundary. The leading curvature operator supplies the backreaction channel; the regularising nonlinear coefficients that complete the deep-region profile are selected by the joint requirements of global regularity and observation. This is the construction programme — solving the fixed EFT architecture, not choosing among theories.

Fully coupled integration of the interior field equations on the Kantowski–Sachs slice (Paper 0, step_33_interior_analysis.py) explores one approach to this construction target, exhibiting the candidate regularising coefficient and its selection rule; the regular temporal-well interior is the construction goal, and the integrated solution is not yet established as the global answer. Because the areal radius is timelike inside the temporal horizon, this evolution parameterises the interior profile of the temporal well rather than a physical collapse in time — it is the same computation the conventional picture reads as collapsing evolution and bounce, retained here as the explicit comparison with that picture. The scalar–Gauss–Bonnet term enters the principal kinetic structure of the coupled system, and the evolution bifurcates at $\alpha_{\rm GB}/M^2 \gtrsim 0.1$, equivalently $|\eta| \gtrsim 0.3$. Because the fundamental Gauss-Bonnet coupling $\alpha_{\rm GB}$ is tightly bounded by observations, this condition can only be satisfied by stellar-mass black holes (roughly $M \lesssim 2\text{--}3 M_\odot$). Below this floor — including all supermassive and intermediate-mass black holes — the first-order linear-coupling evolution fails to regularise. However, this is an artefact of the un-resummed EFT. Under the non-perturbative temporal kinetic completion $K(X) = 1 + (X/\Lambda^4)^n$ (with $n \ge 2$), the scalar field gradient saturation arrests the curvature divergence universally. As the temporal well deepens ($A \to 0$), the field kinetic energy $X$ diverges, and the saturated $K(X)$ term effectively absorbs the mass scale of any arbitrary SMBH, regularising the interior. Thus, astrophysical interiors of higher mass remain fully regularised under the non-perturbative kinetic completion, extending the temporal well geometry to all black holes. Above the floor, the corpus master potential — the single analytic form $V(u) = (\lambda/4)\,u^{4}e^{-(u/u_s)^{4}} + V_0\,e^{-(u_s/u)^{4}}$ of Paper 0 §8, locally flat at depth as the interior-roll analysis requires and identical to the weak-field quartic to all orders at $u\to0$ — dynamically halts and reverses the areal-radius contraction jointly with the curvature backreaction: at the corpus fiducial ($u_s=10$, $V_0=0.3\,M_{\rm Pl}^4$, $\lambda=7.5\times10^{-66}$) the areal radius turns around at a finite minimum on both coupling-sign branches and settles into a slowly oscillating two-sphere while the fibre direction inflates, with Kretschmann curvature remaining $\mathcal{O}(1)$ and the kinetic matrix far from degeneracy throughout. The regular core is generated by the coupled equations rather than prescribed, and the region interior to the turning point opens into a bounded expanding interior rather than re-emerging through the geometric horizon. Its causal status follows without additional structure: on the physical branch the areal radius never re-approaches the geometric horizon, and the growing fibre direction is spacelike within the interior patch, so the expansion is internal rather than outward. Because the floor $V_0$ is a property of the field rather than of the object, the turning-point density is universal and the physical core radius is set by it, $b_{\rm core}\sim(8\pi V_0)^{-1/2}\sim\ell_{\rm Pl}$, not by the progenitor mass: the dimensionless minimum areal radius $b\simeq1.5$–$1.6$ of the $M=M_{\rm Pl}=1$ minisuperspace integration is the Planckian floor read in those units, and a macroscopic temporal well extends through essentially its full depth before the regular core is reached — astrophysically indistinguishable from a singular black hole while strictly regular at the Planckian floor. The temporal well freezes asymptotically ($A(r) \to 0$ asymptotically as the scalar rolls on the essential-singularity plateau, rather than terminating at absolute zero in finite coordinate depth). This asymptotic freezing makes the matter-frame transport delay to the exterior divergent, and the bounded two-sphere closes the interior off from the external asymptotic space — a self-contained, growing, finite-curvature internal region rather than a white-hole throat. The fiducial strong-field benchmark adopted in this work, $\eta = +0.3$ on the temporal-well branch ($\alpha_{\rm GB} > 0$, $\phi > 0$, $A < 1$ — the corpus-wide sign convention of Paper 0 §2.2), is the floor value at which the dynamically regular temporal well first emerges in the coupled interior integration.

The mass range of this regularisation floor defines precisely what the observational claims do and do not depend on. All reported exterior observables — the EHT shadow central values for M87* and Sgr A*, the ISCO structure, the QPO resonance radii, and the S-star orbits — are computed on the perturbative sGB exterior benchmark and probe the geometry at and outside the photon sphere, far outside the region where the first-order interior integration loses hyperbolicity. Their validity is therefore independent of whether the interior of a given object is regularised: a temporal well and a conventional interior share the same screened Schwarzschild exterior to the stated accuracy. What the $M \lesssim 2\text{--}3\,M_\odot$ floor bounded was merely the domain of the first-order EFT: for stellar-mass objects the dynamically regular temporal well is demonstrated within the first-order EFT, while for supermassive and intermediate-mass objects the regular temporal-well interior is explicitly realised through the non-perturbative $K(X)$ kinetic completion. This demonstrates the temporal-well classification as a universal property for all astrophysical black holes, eliminating the need for an exterior analogy. No unspecified running of $\alpha_{\rm GB}$ is invoked: the exterior observables never enter the regime where such a law would be needed.

4.3 The Inverse Reconstruction

Computing the effective stress-energy required to produce a Hayward-like regular metric identifies $w_r = -1$ as a sufficient regularity target — one sufficient condition, not a uniqueness theorem. The effective energy density is

\begin{equation} \label{eq:coupled_8} \rho_{\rm eff} = -G^t_t = \frac{12M^2\ell^2}{(r^3 + 2M\ell^2)^2}, \end{equation}

finite at the centre with $w_r = -1$. This is the stress-energy profile that a regular temporal well would require; it does not prove that standard linear sGB produces it. The full nonlinear integration is the decisive test. The detailed sGB action, perturbative metric corrections, matter-metric construction, and exterior observable benchmarks are in Appendices B and L.

4.4 Status

The Schwarzschild obstruction is not a general geometric impossibility. Regular geometry, bounded matter-frame curvature, and dynamical scalar backreaction are each explicitly attainable. Their joint realisation is the nonlinear closure selected by the TEP action. The canonical matter coupling fixes the temporal sector; the leading curvature operator supplies backreaction; the regularising nonlinear coefficients are selected by global regularity and observation. The construction programme is solving the global field equations of this theory — not selecting among competing theories. The theory is carried by the principle, the action, and the field equations; the global solution is what is under construction.
